The reframe

Pre-decision diligence question: Can rack PDU clear ABB's financial and channel thresholds?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The analysis found insufficient evidence — not a negative finding. Two binding gaps: unit economics (confidence 0.36) and channel access (0.44). No cost model exists. No channel coverage data exists. Asking build/buy/partner is premature. The real question right now: commission a BOM-based margin waterfall and a channel-coverage diagnostic. Only after those close does the original strategic question become answerable.

### Recommendation

Do not green-light yet. The market is real. The proof of economics is not. Commission two workstreams before any entry-mode decision: a BOM-based margin waterfall and a channel-coverage diagnostic against named target accounts.

Weakest link: Unit economics. Confidence sits at 0.36. No bottom-up cost model exists. No blended margin figure has been tested against ABB's 15% IRR hurdle. The IRR sub-hypothesis has confidence 0.10 — the lowest in the entire analysis. Every margin signal in the evidence is qualitative and inferred from Vertiv's public financials, not from ABB's own SKU economics.

What would flip it: Two things would move this to a green light. One: a BOM-based margin waterfall showing blended gross margin above 20% at target volumes, with payback inside three years. Two: a channel-coverage diagnostic confirming IT-reseller relationships at 60%+ of named target accounts within 18 months, or a named acquisition or partner with confirmed approved-vendor status at three or more hyperscalers or Tier-1 colos.

### Framework rationale

GE Market Attractiveness × Make-Buy-Ally (ge-9-box-with-make-buy-ally). This frame is appropriate because the decision has two distinct dimensions: whether the market is attractive enough to enter at all, and which entry mode fits ABB's competitive position. The GE nine-box scores market attractiveness against business strength — directly mapping to the accessible-market and can-win hypotheses. The Make-Buy-Ally overlay then conditions the entry-mode question on where ABB sits in that matrix. The frame also makes explicit that a low business-strength score (driven here by channel absence and unproven unit economics) should block the entry-mode question until strength can be demonstrated or acquired.

### Judgment calls

Margin confidence (0.62) given absence of cost model

The 25–30% gross margin claim rests entirely on Vertiv's public margin expansion commentary and generic intelligent-PDU price-premium statistics. No ABB-specific BOM, no channel-margin leakage estimate, no SKU-mix model exists. The most recent Vertiv cost-pressure disclosure (Q1 2025 risk factors) explicitly flags supply chain and material cost uncertainty.

Intelligent PDU price premiums of 40–60% over basic units are confirmed by a strong third-party source (2025 market data). Sector-level margin feasibility is real. I kept the margin sub-hypothesis at 0.62 rather than lower because the falsifier — blended margin below 20% — is not affirmatively supported by any evidence either. A BOM-based margin waterfall with channel-leakage assumptions would resolve this within weeks.

Brand permission score (0.52) despite ABB's top-5 PDU listing

ABB appears in one market report's top-5 PDU company list, but the same evidence base shows ABB absent from a US PDU manufacturer list and ABB's own internal documents describe rack PDU as a new opportunity under investigation. The gap between being named in a market report and holding formal approved-vendor status at hyperscalers is large and undocumented.

ABB's server room solutions page and global data center product catalog confirm product presence in the electrical-room segment. That is real. I did not conflate product presence with IT-buyer channel access. The falsifier — AVL status at top hyperscalers — is unmet. Interviews with ABB's data center sales leadership and a review of existing framework agreements would resolve this directly.

DC disruption risk scored as moderate rather than high

The 48V-to-400V+ architecture shift is confirmed by Microsoft, Meta, Google, Equinix, and Digital Realty actions in 2024. The red-team challenge on architecture discontinuity is credible. But no source quantifies what percentage of new DC deployments are already bypassing the 48V layer entirely, and the 15% disruption threshold over three years is untested against any penetration model.

The 400–800V shift is concentrated in hyperscale AI infrastructure today. Enterprise and mid-market data centers — a large share of the accessible market — are not yet at that inflection. I weighted the disruption risk as moderate rather than fatal on that basis. A primary survey of non-hyperscale data center operators on their three-year power architecture plans would sharpen this materially.

### How I'd push back on this memo

*   The $50M revenue target assumes ABB can displace incumbents mid-refresh cycle. Vertiv, Schneider, and Eaton hold multi-year enterprise contracts and decade-long distributor relationships in Virginia, Tokyo, and Frankfurt — the exact markets cited as accessible. No signed LOIs, no named pilot customers, and no distributor commitments exist in the evidence. Why would a hyperscaler in Ashburn switch vendors mid-cycle for an ABB product that has never appeared on their AVL?
    
*   The architecture discontinuity between 48V and 400–800V HVDC is not incremental. Microsoft, Meta, and Google began implementing 400–800V systems in April 2024. If new AI data center builds are already bypassing the 48V layer, the product roadmap that 'retains option value' may be optimized for a segment in structural decline. The evidence contains zero market penetration data showing what share of new DC deployments are already on HVDC architectures. That number matters enormously for the three-year investment thesis.
    
*   The IRR sub-hypothesis scored 0.10 — the lowest in the analysis — yet the overall recommendation is 'close the gaps and revisit' rather than 'do not proceed.' A score of 0.10 on the financial viability test is not a data gap; it is an absence of any supporting evidence whatsoever. The burden of proof should be on ABB to produce a cost model before further investment in diligence, not on the analysis to explain why no cost model exists.
